Julien Lam

Permanent CNRS researcher

Welcome! I am Julien Lam, a permanent CNRS Researcher at the Unité Matériaux et Transformations (UMET) in Lille. Building on a research background spanning Lyon, Bristol, Brussels, and Toulouse, my current work focuses on the intersection of Materials Science, Artificial Intelligence, and Molecular Simulation. I specialize in developing machine-learning potentials and rare-event sampling techniques to unravel the complex mechanisms of nanocrystal nucleation. By combining atomistic modeling with AI, my goal for the future is to move toward the rational design of nanomaterials, providing a predictive framework that bridges the gap between fundamental theory and experimental synthesis.

Research interests:
Nucleation, Crystallization, Machine-learning interaction potentials, Free energy sampling, Nanoparticle synthesis

January 7th, 2026

xxxx published on 2026-01-07

Can a system as thoroughly studied as the Lennard-Jones gas still reveal intriguing phenomena? In a collaboration with the Laboratoire de Physique de la Matière Condensée published in Phys Rev Letters, we employed Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations and rare-event sampling to investigate liquid nucleation from the vapor phase. Contrary to the predictions of Classical Nucleation Theory, our results demonstrate that critical clusters nucleate at a density significantly different from that of the macroscopic equilibrium liquid. We rationalize these numerical findings using a two-variable nucleation theory that is both accessible and highly effective.
